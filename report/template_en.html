Smart District Planner

Pick a city and the districts you want, or just how many and let it choose. Within seconds it finds the layout with the highest adjacency bonuses and turns it into map tacks with district icons. It also picks sites for new cities and plans several cities together. Placement rules and adjacency bonuses all come from Detailed Map Tacks (DMT), so the numbers match what the tacks show.

What it does

In Civilization VI a district's yield depends on what surrounds it: Mountains, Rivers, Woods, Rainforest, other districts, wonders. Districts also give each other bonuses, so the first one you place changes where the next one should go. By hand, most players pick good-looking tiles one at a time, and the best combination is hard to see.

  • Many combinations: a city has about 30 usable tiles within 3 rings; 8 districts can be placed in about 308 ≈ 6.6×1011 ways.
  • Many rules: many civilizations change them. Japan's districts get +1 from each other; Korea's Seowon loses yield next to other districts; Gaul's specialty districts may not be next to the City Center; Vietnam's specialty districts must be built on Woods, Rainforest or Marsh.
  • Cities affect each other: neighboring cities can share bonuses and compete for the same tile, and some districts, such as the Government Plaza, are limited to one per empire.
  • What exists stays: districts already built, tacks you placed yourself, and luxury and strategic resources are never moved or overwritten.

This mod takes all of that into account. Adjacency and placement rules from your civilization, leader, pantheon and religion's beliefs are counted with no setup; the weights are your own choice of yields, set with a preset, and do not change with the leader. {{MULTI_LEAD}}

The panel has three tabs: One city, City sites and Several cities. Below is what you set and what you get on each.

The three tabs

One city

You set

  • The city (its name carries a number, the same as on its tacks)
  • Districts, in two groups laid out as a grid; a click cycles the state. Specialty districts go Pool → Must → Off; the others (Aqueduct, Dam, Canal, Neighborhood…) go Auto → Must → Off. Built ones are greyed out; ones at the empire limit or not available to your civilization say why
  • How many specialty districts the city should end up with. Districts built, under construction and map tacks for this city count, and only the rest are planned. Above what the city has plus the Must ones, the solver picks the rest from the pool; next to it, your specialty district slots (built, planned now, the current population limit), with a warning only when you go over
  • Wonders to place along, as many as you like, shown as tags; the dropdown lists them by era, then by cost
  • Weights (0–3): raise a yield and the plan leans toward it; Tiles keeps districts off good tiles, the distance cost keeps them near the City Center, Housing values a Neighborhood's Housing, Reach is as on the Several cities tab. Presets: Balanced, Science, Culture, Religion. How each is counted: the install and use guide
  • Land loss: each tile valued with its best improvement, chopping and harvesting first. Switches: also use tiles swapped from your other cities (on by default; at equal value no swap); allow removing Woods, Rainforest and Marsh; replace this mod's earlier tacks for this city; reminder tacks. A Harbor and a Commercial Hub are always kept in different cities, with nothing to set

You get

  • The weighted gain, split into adjacency, land, distance, reach, and housing and appeal; a district with a reach says how many cities it newly covers
  • Neighborhood and Preserve: their tile's Appeal and the Housing it gives (such as “Housing 6 (Appeal 4)”); for a Preserve also the yields its Grove and Sanctuary give the tiles around it (scored when it is a Must; picked from the pool it is shown, marked “pool pick, not scored”). Other districts of the plan change Appeal too (Industrial Zone −1, Holy Site +1, removing Woods…), and that is counted
  • When picking from the pool: three plans with different districts, each saying which it picked; click one to preview its icons and bonuses on the map
  • One row per district and wonder: its tile and bonus (such as “Sci+2 Cul+1”); the first line of its tooltip is the district's own score (its weighted adjacency, with the tiles it takes and its distance from the City Center in brackets, such as “This district: weighted adjacency +4 (tiles -2.5; distance -1)”), then DMT's breakdown; added and picked districts are marked
  • Hovering the summary: the adjacency by yield (before and after the weights), and what is not in any district's line
  • An Aqueduct added when the city lacks fresh water, a Dam when there are 3 or more Floodplains tiles within 3 rings (Gathering Storm); their tiles are solved as usual
  • When the plan gets adjacency from adjacent Woods or Rainforest: those tiles and what clearing them would cost; placing the plan also puts a reminder tack on them
  • Existing districts it affects: their weighted score before and after
  • Districts left out and why: no valid tile in range, already built, at the empire limit, not available to your civilization, too few in the pool
  • When a Harbor and a Commercial Hub share a city (both Must, or no other way to reach the count): a note that the Lighthouse then gives no Trade Route capacity
  • “Good places”: pick a district and the map colors every tile it can go on in four bands by its bonus there, with the number

City sites

You set

  • How many new cities, and how far they may be
  • Dense packing: as many new cities as fit (off by default): the plan that fits more new cities comes first, then the one with the higher score; New cities is then 12 by default (at most 12), kept apart from the usual one together with Max distance
  • A weight preset (Balanced, Growth first, Districts first), each weight adjustable, in two rows: the six yields and the distance cost; then the parts of a site's score: Districts, Tiles, Features, Housing, Luxuries, Strategics and Appeal (Appeal off by default)
  • The districts used to estimate a site's district potential, as Must, Pool or Off like on the One city tab, and a count (3 by default, at most 6): every site is scored with the Must ones and picks the rest from the pool, best district score first. By default every specialty district is in the pool except the Preserve, Encampment and Aerodrome (no adjacency bonus and no Amenities); an empire-limited one such as the Government Plaza does not count per site

You get

  • Three site plans, each with its total, and how many candidate sites were looked at and how many scored with districts
  • For each site: its points per part (each part is worked out as yields per turn, a typical good site gets 10 points, times the part's weight), with the yields per turn behind each part and what earns 10 points in its tooltip; its water (Fresh Water, Coastal Water or No Water); how far it is from your nearest city or placed site tack and from the other sites of the plan; the luxury and strategic resources it gets; its features and the districts it picked
  • Risk notes: Loyalty (foreign cities near), natural disasters (Floodplains, Volcanic Soil), unrevealed tiles within 3 rings, strategic resources not visible yet
  • A preview of each site's 3 rings on the map, then site tacks (numbered after your existing cities); with dense packing each site has a tick box, Select all ticks them all, and only the ticked sites get tacks

Several cities

You set

  • The cities that take part: every site tack, plus your cities within 7 tiles of a new one (8 at most). Each can be unticked: an unticked site tack is not planned but stays as a neighbor; an unticked city gets no new districts but its districts still count as neighbors
  • District lists: one shared by all cities, or a city's own; the grid is the same as on the One city tab. Per city: how many districts each city should end up with, counting what it has; a city that has enough picks nothing more from the pool. “Apply a template” fills a list for Science, Production or another yield from your civilization's rules; it can also be cleared or set back to the shared one
  • Empire-limited districts (Government Plaza and the like): written into one city's list, they go in that city; into several, the solver picks among them
  • Wonders (tags and a dropdown), yield, Tiles and Housing weights, the distance cost, presets as on the One city tab; switches: replace this mod's earlier tacks for the cities taking part, reminder tacks; Harbor and Commercial Hub kept apart city by city, as on the One city tab
  • A coverage weight (0–3, default 0, off): Factories, Power Plants, Zoos, Stadiums, Aquariums and wonders such as the Colosseum help your cities within some distance (mostly 6 tiles in the rules); the plan counts the cities it newly covers, also when picking from the pool; the One city tab has the same column

You get

  • Three clearly different plans, each with its total, the number of cities, districts and wonders, and each city's weighted adjacency from its new districts; the results have a subtotal row for each city, and district rows have the same tooltips as on the One city tab
  • Bonuses across cities and empire limits counted; a tile two cities could use goes to one of them
  • When picking from the pool, each city's picked districts are marked; a city that cannot fit enough from its pool says so; Aqueducts and Dams are added as on the One city tab
  • With a coverage weight: the total lists coverage on its own, and a district or wonder with a reach says how many cities it newly covers (hover for their names)
  • A comparison with planning the cities one at a time on the One city tab (in the hover statistics)
  • A preview of every district's icon and bonus on the map, then tacks

Install and use

  1. Subscribe on the Steam Workshop to Smart District Planner and to Detailed Map Tacks (DMT), which it needs.
  2. Without the Workshop: get the zip from the release page and put the SmartDistrictPlanner folder in it into the game's Mods folder: on Windows Documents\My Games\Sid Meier's Civilization VI\Mods\, on macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Sid Meier's Civilization VI/Sid Meier's Civilization VI/Mods/. To update, delete the old folder first. DMT is still needed from the Workshop. When you switch to the Workshop, delete this folder: with both, the game loads only one of them, not necessarily the newer one.
  3. Enable: in the main menu's Additional Content, tick Detailed Map Tacks and Smart District Planner, then restart the game.
  4. Open: in a game, click the hexagon button at the top left of the screen, or press Shift+L.

Two common ways to use it:

  • Choose sites for new cities, then plan them with the cities near them: City sites tab: choose, preview, place site tacks → Several cities tab: plan districts and wonders for the new cities and the cities nearby → preview → place tacks.
  • Add or change districts in a city you have: One city tab: plan the city → place tacks; if you do not like it, undo, or tick “replace this mod's earlier tacks” and plan again.

Notes

  • One planner per game: Auto Map Tacks Planner, Empire Planner and other planners each simulate, create and delete map tacks, and with two enabled they disturb each other (section 3). Mods that change adjacency or district rules can also make the results differ from what you see.
  • Rulesets and languages: works with the base game, Rise and Fall, and Gathering Storm; Canals and Dams appear only in Gathering Storm. The panel is in Chinese and English and follows the game's language.
  • Saves: the tacks are ordinary map tacks; saves still load with the mod disabled.
  • Multiplayer has not been tested.

Known limitations

  • The only improvements it plans are the ones a wonder must stand next to (a Camp for the Temple of Artemis, tacked with the temple); for the rest a tile is valued by the best improvement it can take, but no improvement tack is placed.
  • The Several cities tab has no switch to forbid removing features: it always allows removing Woods, Rainforest and Marsh.
  • Each kind of district is planned once; for several Neighborhoods, solve several times.
  • Appeal: improvements built later outside the plan are not in the model; a Preserve's yields on the tiles around it assume those tiles stay unimproved; a tacked Preserve counts only its Housing. In the late-game check, all 9 Neighborhood tiles have the game's Appeal and Housing (appendix B.7).
  • A Preserve's yields on the tiles around it count only when it is a Must: with many Breathtaking and Charming tiles they can reach 40–70 points, but they come only with a Grove and a Sanctuary built; counting them when picking from the pool would make the pool almost always pick the Preserve first. So from the pool only its Housing counts, and those yields are shown.
  • Placing many tacks at once: DMT updates each new tack and the tacks around it, reading and writing its whole tack table each time, so it slows down as tacks add up. With about 100 tacks on the map, placing 80 more keeps the game busy for about 12 seconds; a normal batch of 15 takes about 0.25 seconds.
  • Picking from the pool compares only the objective (adjacency and Housing, plus land, coverage and distance when on); the yields of a district's buildings are not counted, so districts without adjacency, such as the Encampment and Aerodrome, are never picked from the pool; set them to Must when you want them.
  • Reminder tacks are only for Woods and Rainforest next to the planned districts; features next to existing districts have nothing to do with this plan.
  • City site scores are estimates: tiles, resources and district potential are judged from what is revealed now; the districts are planned exactly in the Several cities step.
  • Canals: DMT does not check that a Canal connects water. On tiles the city owns, the engine check catches it; on tiles it does not own yet, the game gives no answer, so DMT decides there and a Canal that does not connect water is possible.
  • Shipwrecks: DMT counts a shipwreck on water as a sea resource, so a Harbor next to one shows 1 Gold more than the game gives; this mod's bonus comes from DMT and counts it too. Shipwrecks become visible late, so the effect is small.
  • Multiplayer has not been tested.

Design goals

Six design goals. Each algorithm below is marked with the goals it serves, and appendix B tests them.

  • G1The numbers are DMT'sThe score used in planning and the number on the map tack come from the same DMT code; the mod writes no rules of its own.
  • G2As close to the best as possible, and say how closeSmall problems are solved exhaustively; large ones get an upper bound and a quality note, and “Improve” searches further.
  • G3Only valid plans that can be builtAdds the game rules DMT does not check: resources, empire limits, the city's range and ownership.
  • G4Respect what the player hasBuilt districts and the player's tacks are never moved or overwritten, and count as neighbors; every requested district that fits is placed.
  • G5Same input, same resultNo random numbers, a fixed order of work, budgets counted in steps rather than seconds.
  • G6Never freeze the gameLong computations run over several frames, and closing the panel does not stop them.

The mod is a pure Lua UI mod and does not touch the game's C++. Placement rules and adjacency bonuses are left to DMT (G1); the planner only searches for which district goes on which tile, and adds the game's rules DMT does not check (G3, see A.7 and A.8). All solvers share one search engine and one evaluation layer.

A.8Placement: three checks

G3

Whether a district can go on a tile is decided by three checks in turn; failing any of them keeps the tile out of the plan. Every “can it go here” question goes through the same entry of the overlay, CanPlace, so One city, Several cities, the district estimate for City sites and Good places all use the same checks.

  1. Candidate tiles (when the city is scanned): within the city's 3 rings, revealed, not a tile of another civilization or of another of your cities; no City Center, district, wonder, natural wonder or existing tack on it; no luxury or strategic resource that cannot be removed (DMT removes the resource before it evaluates a district tack, so it cannot catch this); with Allow chop off, tiles whose feature would be removed are skipped. Empire-limited districts count the ones built plus the ones tacked.
  2. DMT's CanPlacePin: terrain, features, adjacency requirements, and placement rules from civilization abilities (such as Gaul and Vietnam).
  3. Engine check: on tiles the city owns, the game engine gives the list of tiles where the district can be built (CityManager.GetOperationTargets, which the game's placement lens uses too); any tile not on the list is refused. When the list is empty (the district is not unlocked, or there is no free slot) or the tile does not belong to the city yet, DMT decides. Each (city, district) pair is asked once per turn.

A.9Map tacks: numbers, records, replacing and undo

G1 G4

Tacks are made the same way DMT's own popup makes them, so DMT shows their bonuses as usual; a tack is named with its city's number (existing cities 1, 2, … in the order they were founded, new sites after them). Each batch of tacks is recorded in the player's configuration and kept with the save: each tack's ID, tile, icon and name. Only a tack with all four unchanged counts as this mod's; one you changed or deleted is left alone. With “Replace this mod's earlier tacks” ticked, the solve request carries these tacks: the scan does not treat them as locked, and the overlay leaves them out of the copy of the data it gives DMT (the real data stays as it is); placing the new plan deletes the old tacks first, the batch records what it replaced, and undo rebuilds them as they were. The code keeps only tack IDs and fetches the tack again each time it is used: after a new tack is made in the game, a tack object fetched earlier may be invalid, and calling it crashes the game.

B.1Method

To be repeatable and to cover many situations, the quality evaluation runs offline, without starting the game:

  • Real rules: districts, adjacency, terrain, features, resources, civilization and leader abilities and modifiers all come from the Gathering Storm rules database the game itself exports, not from a simplified version written by hand.
  • Real code: the Lua code of DMT and of this mod is loaded as it is, without any change; this mod runs in a Lua state of its own (as its UI context in the game does), and the panel's buttons are clicked as a player would.
  • A simulated engine: the map, cities, map tacks, visibility, rivers and Floodplains, and civilization modifiers (GameEffects) are simulated by the test framework; every game function the planner can call is implemented with the game's meaning.
  • A separate judge: once a plan is placed as map tacks, a separate state that loads only DMT scores it; that is, the number the player sees on the tacks. It also checks the game's rules: DMT's placement rules, no district on a resource, the city's range and ownership, one per city, empire limits, population slots, whether a tack of the player is overwritten, and whether every requested district is placed.
  • Against the true optimum: the true optimum of every scenario is found separately by branch and bound, also scored by DMT (see “True optimum” below). Each scenario sets a district list and yield weights (all 1, except in “other weights”), and the judge scores with the same weights.

Measures

  • Adjacency: once the plan is placed as tacks, the sum of DMT adjacency over all of the player's districts and district tacks (by the scenario's yield weights), minus the same sum before planning.
  • At the optimum / quality: the reference of each scenario is its proven true optimum (see “True optimum” below); where it cannot be proven (mostly large cities of 10–12 districts, and 3–4 cities together) the reference is the best plan known, with an upper bound on the optimum. A plan equal to the optimum is “at the optimum” (ties allowed); quality = adjacency ÷ reference.
  • Violations: DMT says the tile is not valid; on a luxury or strategic resource; beyond the city's 3 rings; on a tile of another civilization or of another of your cities; twice in a city; over the empire limit; over the population slots; a tack of the player overwritten.
  • Left out: a requested district missing from the plan (not a violation, but counted in completeness). Whether it was left out although it had a valid tile is decided by the judge: after the plan is placed as tacks, the city still has a free slot, and within its 3 rings there is a tile DMT accepts that breaks none of the rules above.
  • Coverage: how many of the player's cities an Industrial Zone and an Entertainment Complex cover (a city counts when its City Center lies within the district's radius; the two kinds counted apart, each kind once per city), taking what the plan adds. The judge computes it on its own from the buildings' radius in the rules data.
  • Determinism: whether two runs of the same scenario with the same input give exactly the same plan.

True optimum

  • How: branch and bound over the candidate tiles and validity rules of this mod's search, every plan scored by the same DMT. The upper bound is each district's value placed alone plus the pair value of every two neighbouring districts; a pair value is the larger of DMT's measured value on those two tiles and the rules table, rules such as “+1 for every two neighbours” count by their share, and the pairs among districts not placed yet are no more than the edges that many hexagons can have. A wonder is a slot like a district; when districts are picked from a pool, every combination is searched in turn.
  • Self-checks: {{ORACLE_CHECK_TEXT}}
  • Where it cannot be proven: most large cities of 10–12 districts are not proven within 5 million nodes, and 3–4 cities together are larger still; they get a range: the lower end is the best plan known (for large cities, the best of every offline run and the branch and bound; for several cities, the Thorough plan continued 20 times, then 200,000 steps of simulated annealing, then improved by the branch and bound), the upper end the largest bound of what the branch and bound did not search.
  • Open to checking: each scenario's result carries the optimal plan, its value, whether it is proven and the nodes searched, plus fingerprints of the rules database, DMT and the code, so anyone can check it with DMT; scenarios are generated from fixed seeds, and besides the development and held-out sets there is a sealed set run only for releases.

B.8Limits

In three groups: checked already, being planned, and not planned for now.

Checked already

  • The engine is simulated: map generation, cities, visibility and the modifier system are implemented by the test framework; the rules data and the code of this mod and DMT are unchanged. Two checks: scenarios exported from real saves (RS, RM) compared tile by tile with the game, 0 differences in DMT's verdicts; the in-game engine audit on the Continents save, 1,361 of 1,368 pairs agreeing with the engine, the other 7 all Canals (section 5). The simulated engine gives no buildable tiles, so the offline evaluation has no engine check.
  • The judge: DMT and the game engine give the same adjacency tile by tile (B.6); the only known difference is shipwrecks (section 5).
  • The optimum is over this mod's valid tiles: tiles DMT accepts but this mod rules out by the game's rules (on a resource, outside the territory and so on, appendix A.8) are outside the search for the optimum.

Being planned

  • Large cities and several cities have ranges only: for large cities of 10–12 districts and for 3–4 cities together no optimum is proven, and section 4 gives a range; tightening the bound further to prove more of them is not done yet.
  • The main measure is adjacency only: the panel's default objective also counts tile potential and missing Housing, and a plan sometimes gives up some adjacency for them: in the 84 development scenarios, the Standard plan with Land loss on has less adjacency than the adjacency-only plan in 31, on average 94% of it and 62% at the lowest. “City output” counts the best tiles the citizens work as they are now, not what they give once improved, so it does not show this either: in the same 84 scenarios the plan with Land loss on has the higher city output in 23 and the lower in 15, about the same on average. The judge also reports the potential output with tiles valued as improved, and the true optimum of the panel's default objective (adjacency + tiles + Housing) has been found for {{N_LAND_ORACLE}} one-city scenarios (design record docs/notes/issue-48-land-oracle.md); the main measure of this page is still adjacency.
  • Time: offline times differ from times in the game, so only relative speed counts; every result also records the number of DMT calls, which does not depend on the machine; this page does not use it yet.
  • Few real scenarios: the real set is 14 one-city and 5 several-city scenarios of Japan and the Netherlands from two saves, and the case for turning “Swap tiles from my cities” on by default (how much adjacency it adds, how many tiles need a swap) comes from them; the reference layouts rebuilt from community saves (docs/reference-scenes.md) are used to set the weights and are not part of this page's evaluation.
  • Weights: the evaluation uses the default weights, all 1, plus three sets leaning to Science, Production or Faith; what the default weights should be is in the objective and weights milestone.
  • Improvements: this mod only values tiles by their best improvement and places no improvement tacks, so the evaluation does not look at improvements. The improvement a wonder must stand next to (a Camp for the Temple of Artemis) is planned with the wonder; the 8 WA scenes of the offline evaluation check that the Camp is valid.

Not planned for now

  • Rulesets: the offline evaluation uses only the Gathering Storm rules database; Rise and Fall and the base game are tested only in the game (all three rulesets once each). The solving code is the same for all three; they differ in the rules data.
  • Test setup: the in-game tests run only on one Mac with the maintainer's DLC set (16 DLC missing); on Windows a collaborator tests by hand.

Code versions: DMT 2024-01-17, the Workshop version at the time of testing (October 2026).
